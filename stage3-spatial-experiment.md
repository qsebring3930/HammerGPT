Coordinates use abstract distance units; no CS unit/speed conversion is assumed. A conditional rear-flank relationship can reuse the defender transfer's physical composition rather than create a literal new link.

`spatial_validation.py` reports route lengths/ratios, observed entry directions, convergence positions, overlaps, layering and rotation directness. It checks route/place/relationship identity, endpoint levels, ordering implied by existing duration contracts, independently accessible Mid and two-destination reachability. Coarse raster navigation treats footprint overlap as movement availability regardless of team/phase labels; it flags short or safe paths avoiding the proposed transfer. Its cell size is a numerical resolution parameter, not a tactical threshold. Timing-order implications remain conditional on how travel versus combat/clearance durations are allocated.

Spatial equivalence uses normalized Procrustes alignment with reflection plus region-boundary, travel-curve and layer comparisons. Minor-jitter bounds come from the declared uncertainty of approximate territories, not a CS quality cutoff. Primary landmarks are compared separately so recovery appendages cannot be the sole basis for a novelty claim. Exact translation, rotation, reflection, uniform scaling, combined transforms and minor-jitter controls are retained separately from the sixteen generated candidates.
